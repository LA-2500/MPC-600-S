Chapter 1Introduction

Welcome

The MPC 600 S is a pad-based sampler and sequencer for the Akai MPC Sample, built around sequences, tracks, programs and sounds. It has S600 programs based on Akai’s S-series samplers, an arpeggiator with step patterns, warping and timestretching, effects for the input, the tracks and the master output, stem separation, and a sampler that records at low sample rates and bit depths.

It runs from the SD card. Nothing is flashed to the MPC Sample, and the original Akai software is left untouched: remove the card and the MPC Sample starts as it came.

Installing and Starting

The MPC 600 S comes as mpc-os.zip, an autorun package for the MPC Sample’s SD card:

  1. Unzip it. Copy the autorun folder inside to the top level of an SD card (FAT32 or exFAT), its name and files unchanged.
  2. With the MPC Sample off, put the card in and turn it on. The MPC Sample starts as usual; a few seconds later the MPC 600 S takes over the screen. The title screen shows the version, then the main sequence page.
  3. To go back to the stock MPC Sample, turn it off and take the card out. To update, replace the autorun folder with the new one.

The separate Quick Start guide has these steps in full, with what to check if it does not start.

The OS keeps everything it saves in a folder named MPC600S at the top of the card, outside the autorun package, so updating the OS never touches your work:

Knobs and the Slider Catch Up

The knobs and slider are absolute: each has a position. When you turn one, it does not change the value until it reaches the value’s own position; from there it takes over. Nothing jumps when you first touch a control.

Zoomed in on a waveform (hold SAMPLE and press B1 or B2), the Zone and Loop point knobs change behaviour: they move the point from where it is, in fine steps of 1/127 of what is on screen. The zoomed view follows the point you moved last (Start or End, Loop St or Loop End) and keeps it in the middle of the screen, even at the very beginning or end of the sound, where the view runs on past it.

Pressing a Page’s Button Again

On every page, pressing the button that opened it again puts the cursor back on the page’s first field (on a few pages, on the field you will want most, such as Effect # on Input Effects).

Help

Press SHIFT + SAMPLE REC on any page to see that page’s help, and SHIFT + SEQ REC to see its second page. Where there are more, B1 <Prev> and B3 <Next> turn the pages and B2 <Exit> goes back. The help tells you what the page does, the keys you cannot see (the SHIFT combinations), and tips. A small helper stands at the side of the help pages, tapping her foot and pointing at the text with her stick.

The main sequence page has no help: there, the same two buttons move the play position back and forward a bar.

Undo and Redo

SHIFT + − undoes and SHIFT + + redoes, up to two steps. Undo covers audio commands (Discard, Convert, Print Effects, Warp and the like) and changes to the sequences (recording, erasing, editing bars). It does not undo settings such as a pad’s volume.

Definitions

  • Sound: a recording in memory: a sample.
  • Pad: one of 128 places in a program (16 pads in each of 8 banks), each with a sound and that sound’s settings.
  • Program: a whole set of 128 pads. There are 24. A program is MPC (drums) or S600 (a playable synth instrument, Chapter 9).
  • Memory: up to 64 sounds kept without being on a pad. A sound that leaves its last pad goes here instead of being lost.
  • Sequence: a piece of music up to 999 bars long. There are 99.
  • Track: one part of a sequence. Each sequence has 99 tracks, DRUM (plays a program) or MIDI (sends notes out).
  • Song: a list of sequences played in order. There are 20.

Playing the Pads

Pads play the active track’s program, through that track’s mixer channel. Full Level (SHIFT + Pad 1) plays every hit at full velocity.

16 Levels

LEVELS lays one sound across the 16 pads: Velocity (pad 1 softest to pad 16 hardest), then Tune (a chromatic scale, the root pad playing the sound’s own pitch), then off. SHIFT + LEVELS opens its page, where you choose the type, the sound and the root (D13 by default). In Tune, PAD BANK reaches further up and down: 128 tunings in all.

Chapter 3Recording Sequences

The Main Page

SEQ opens the main page: Play/Record at the top (sequence, tempo, bars, time signature, loop) and Track Data under it (track, type, on/off, MIDI channel, velocity %, program). The Now: line shows the position as bar.beat.tick.

The knobs are labelled at the bottom: K1 the track, K2 the tempo (whole BPM), K3 the track’s program. The slider steps through the tracks. B1 turns the track on or off; B2 and B3 step to the track before and after. SHIFT + B3 opens Copy Sequence (Chapter 6).

Renaming

Put the cursor on Seq, Trk or Pgm and press SHIFT + B2 to rename the sequence, the track or the track’s program. The wheel sets the highlighted letter, − and + move along the name (up to 16 letters), B3 <OK> keeps it and B1 <Cancel> leaves the old name. A MIDI track’s Pgm is a program number, so it has no name to change.

{{shot:seq_main_knobs|The main page}}

Recording

  1. Choose the sequence and the track to record on, on the main page.
  2. Press SEQ REC to arm a recording mode (see below). The light flashes while armed, and the display shows the mode.
  3. Press PLAY. If Count-in is on, the count-in comes first.
  4. Play the pads, or a MIDI keyboard. Each note goes onto the active track, placed by Timing Correct (Chapter 4), with its velocity and how long you held it.
  5. Press STOP. SHIFT + − undoes the whole recording.

The Recording Modes

Pressed while stopped, SEQ REC steps through three settings: OVERDUB, then REC, then off. OVERDUB comes first because it never erases anything.

Punching In and Out

While the sequence is playing, press SEQ REC to punch in: recording starts there and then, in OVERDUB, without stopping or erasing anything. Press it again to punch out: the sequence keeps playing and recording stops. Undo covers a punch-in too.

Looping, Erasing and Holding Notes

  • Loop on the main page sends the sequence back to a bar at the end, so you can keep recording pass after pass.
  • Hold ERASE and hold pads while recording or playing: those pads’ notes are erased as the sequence passes them. Let go to stop erasing.
  • Hold NOTE REPEAT while recording: the repeats or the arpeggio are recorded as notes (Chapter 5).
  • A note’s length is how long you held the pad. With a sustain pedal set to it on MIDI Settings, the pedal holds notes on.

Recording Knobs and the Slider

While recording, moves of the knobs and slider on Volume, Pan, Cutoff, Resonance, Semi and Fine are recorded as well, on drum sounds and on S600 programs. They play back with the sequence, so a filter sweep or a pitch drop becomes part of the part. Holding ERASE with a pad also erases that pad’s recorded moves as the sequence passes them.

Tempo, Bars and Length

  • Tap Tempo: tap the button in time. SHIFT + TAP TEMPO opens the metronome page (click on/off, in play, volume, rate, count-in bars and type).
  • Bars: changing Bars on the main page asks first when it matters. Adding bars to a sequence with notes asks Duplicate sequence data?: Yes repeats the existing bars from bar 1 into the new ones, No leaves them empty. Taking bars away when the removed bars have notes asks Delete bars x-y? first.
  • Double and half the sequence: SHIFT + Pad 3 and SHIFT + Pad 2 do the same with one press.
  • Double and half speed: SHIFT + Pad 7 and SHIFT + Pad 6 double or halve the BPM, asking Apply note-value compensation?. No changes only the BPM, so the music plays faster or slower. Yes keeps it playing at the same speed: doubling moves every note twice as far along and doubles the bars; halving squeezes the music into the first half and repeats it in the second.

Audio Tracks

The MPC 600 S records audio onto a pad in time with the sequence, so a vocal, a guitar or a synth part plays back with the music as an audio track. The take is an ordinary sound on a pad, started by a note at the beginning of a track, so it can be edited, effected, warped and mixed like any other.

Recording an Audio Track

  1. Press SAMPLE REC twice for the Audiotrack Record page.
  2. Set the Input (LINE or MIC), the Mode (stereo or one of the mono choices), Count In (2 bars by default, or 1) and Length.
  3. Select the track the take should go on, on the main page beforehand (a DRUM track).
  4. Press <Rec>. The sequence plays from its start after the count-in, and the take begins exactly on the first beat of bar 1.
  5. When it ends (or you press STOP or <Stop>), press an empty pad. It gets the take, and a note at the very start of the active track, as long as the take, starts it whenever the sequence plays from the top.

How It Stays in Time

  • The take is lined up with the sequence’s first tick, not with the moment you pressed <Rec>: the count-in is cut off.
  • The time the sound takes to get through the MPC Sample’s input is measured and taken out, so a part you played along to the click lands where you played it.
  • The pad needs to be empty: the take never overwrites a sound. A full pad asks you to choose again.
  • On a MIDI track the take still goes on the pad, but no note is added: select a DRUM track to have it play with the sequence.

Working With Audio Tracks

  • Record several takes onto different pads, and mute or solo them on Track Mute (PAD MUTE) to pick the best.
  • Put each take on a separate track to give it a mixer channel, EQ and effect sends.
  • Use Warp (Chapter 12) to fit a take to a new tempo, and Print Effects to commit an effect to it.
  • If the sequence plays from the middle, the take does not start: it is started by its note at the beginning, as any one-shot sound would be.

Chapter 4Timing Correct and the Swing Algorithms

Timing Correct

SHIFT + Pad 14 opens the Timing Correct page; SHIFT + Pad 10 turns it off and back on. As you record, each note moves to the nearest step of the Note value: 1/4, 1/4T, 1/8, 1/8T, 1/16, 1/16T, 1/32, 1/32T, 1/64, 1/64T, or OFF. Shift timing then moves every note a few ticks earlier or later. Move Existing applies all of this to notes already recorded, over a range of bars, for all notes or the pads you press.

{{shot:timing_correct|The Timing Correct page}}

How Swing Works

Swing works on pairs of steps. With a 1/16 note value, each pair of 1/16 notes makes an 1/8 note; the first note of the pair stays where it is and the second is moved later. The Swing % says how far through the pair the second note lands: 50% is straight (half-way), 66% is a triplet feel (two thirds of the way), and 75% is the most.

Swing applies to the straight note values 1/8, 1/16 and 1/32. The triplet values are never swung. It applies to notes as they are recorded, to Move Existing, and to Note Repeat and the arpeggiator as they play.

The sequencer counts 96 ticks per quarter note (96 PPQN). A 1/16 note is 24 ticks, an 1/8 is 48, a 1/32 is 12. At 90 BPM one tick lasts about 6.9 milliseconds.

The MPC 600 S has four swing timings, because the way a Swing % becomes a delay differs from machine to machine, and the difference is audible. Sequencer Res on Additional Settings (Chapter 15) chooses which: MPC 3000, MPC60, MPC 2000 or SP12.

MPC 3000: Fixed Steps

The MPC 3000 setting moves in fixed steps: the swung 1/32 note moves a whole number of ticks, from 0 to 6, and the delay doubles for each larger note value.

1/32 delay = (Swing − 50) × 12 ÷ 50, rounded to the nearest whole tick (0 to 6)
1/16 delay = 2 × the 1/32 delay    1/8 delay = 4 × the 1/32 delay

So at 1/16 every setting lands on an even number of ticks, and 66% gives exactly 8 ticks: a perfect 2:1 triplet. Settings in between snap to the nearest step: 52% is still straight at 1/16, for example.

MPC60: As Measured

The MPC60 setting places notes where grooves recorded from a real MPC60 put them, at every setting from 50 to 75. At 1/16 it moves one tick at a time, where the MPC 3000 moves two, so the two part by a tick at 11 of the 26 settings. Its one surprise is 64, which lands on 6 ticks, the same as 63, where an even progression would give 7. It was measured that way, and it plays that way.

Chapter 5Note Repeat and the Arpeggiator

Note Repeat

Hold NOTE REPEAT and hold pads: they repeat at the Rate (1/4 to 1/32T), swung at 1/8, 1/16 and 1/32. Press harder for louder. While NOTE REPEAT is held, B1–B3 switch to another rate (the page shows which); B Keys sets whether they work while held (HOLD) or stay on until pressed again (TOGGLE). SHIFT + NOTE REPEAT opens the page; B2 <Rate> steps the rate.

The Arpeggiator

Set Type to ARPEGGIATOR and the held pads play one at a time in a pattern instead. Hold NOTE REPEAT and the pads as before.

The knobs work from where they are, a step for each small turn: K1 the Rate, K2 the Category, K3 the Pattern. B2 <Rate> still steps the rate, and B3 <Edit> opens Pattern Edit.

Step Patterns

A step pattern is up to 16 steps, each played at the Rate from the lowest pad held. Each step is a note, an octave, an accent, a slide, a tie or a rest: enough for basslines, sequenced leads and the squelching, sliding lines of acid house.

Step settingWhat it does
NoteC to B, counted up from the pitch of the lowest pad held: C is that pad’s own pitch.
Oct−1, 0 or +1: the note an octave down or up.
TimeNOTE plays the step. TIE plays nothing and holds the note before on through this step. REST is silence.
AccentThe step at full velocity (the pad’s pressure). Steps without an accent play at 70% of it, so accents stand out.
SlideThe note holds on and glides into the next note over about 60 ms, without starting again: its envelopes carry on. A slide works on any sound, drum pads included; over MIDI it overlaps the notes (legato) for the synth to glide.

A note without a slide sounds for the Gate’s share of its step (and of any ties after it). Patterns can be any length from 1 to 16 steps; a pattern of 12 steps against the 16 of a bar shifts every pass.

Squelch, Rubber Bounce, Jackin, Deep Ties, Phrygian, Roller, 3 Over 4 (12 steps), Sunny, Slider, Robot, Stairs, Off Kilter (15 steps), Ping, Snake, Siren, Hypno (10 steps). They are written from C; play them from any pad and Trans moves them.

  • Anywhere else (another category, or Type set to NOTE REPEAT), it switches to the arpeggiator on the USER pattern you edited last (USER 1 the first time) and opens it.

Chapter 6Editing Sequences

Erase

While playing, hold ERASE and press pads: those pads’ notes are erased as the sequence passes them. Chapter 9S600 Programs

What an S600 Program Is

An S600 program plays its pads as instruments rather than as drums. Set a program’s Type to S600 on Program Config and every pad in it gets a second set of settings, its S600 settings, kept apart from its drum settings. The pad’s sound, trim, loop, volume and tuning are shared; everything else is the S600’s own. Switch the program back to MPC and its drum settings are exactly as you left them.

On an S600 program, SAMPLE opens the S600 pages instead of the drum pages, MIDI keys play the selected pad chromatically from note 60, and 16 Levels Tune plays it across the pads.

Structure

An S600 program makes a sample into a playable instrument, laid out the way Akai’s S-series samplers did it. A sample feeds a filter; an amplitude envelope and a separate filter envelope shape it; an LFO adds vibrato and movement; velocity, pitch bend and modulation respond; it plays polyphonically or monophonically with portamento; and more than one sample can sound on each note.

Four single-cycle oscillator waveforms come with the MPC 600 S: SINE, SAW, SQUARE and PULSE. Each is one cycle, 674 points at 44.1 kHz, which plays at C2. At power-on the MPC 600 S puts them on the card in MPC600S/s600 (any that are missing; ones you have changed are kept).

The S600 LPF

The default filter on an S600 program is the S600 LPF, a four-pole low-pass modelled on the transistor ladder of the Minimoog Model D: 24 dB per octave, a resonant peak that grows steadily (about +14 dB at 80% resonance and +17 dB at 95%), self-oscillation at the top of the range, low end that holds up as resonance rises, and saturation that thickens and squashes loud signals. This is deliberately not an S-series filter: it is there because it sounds good on synth basses and leads. LPF to BPF 4 on an S600 program are the same filters as on an MPC program.

Sample Rate and Bit Depth

The rate and depth are the sound itself. A sound recorded at 26.04 kHz and 12 bits is stored as 26,040 twelve-bit samples a second. There is no full-quality copy underneath with an effect on top: what you hear is what was sampled. Nor is any particular sampler’s circuitry modelled. The character comes from the rate and the depth.

How a Take Is Made

  1. Capture. The MPC Sample’s converter digitises the input at 44.1 kHz, 16 bits. The OS holds the take as 32-bit floating point from here on, so nothing is lost between steps.
  2. Pitch (if set). The input is sped up by 2p/12 for p semitones (0 to +24) before it is sampled.
  3. Sampling at your rate. The new sound’s samples are taken at the exact moments a converter running at your chosen rate would take them. The input’s value at each of those moments, which falls between the 44.1 kHz samples, is reconstructed from the samples around it by band-limited interpolation (see the table below).
  4. Anti-Aliasing. With it ON, the interpolation’s cutoff is set to 45% of the new sample rate, so it is also a steep low-pass in front of the new rate. With it OFF, the cutoff stays at 45% of 44.1 kHz: everything the capture holds up to about 19 kHz is sampled, and whatever lies above half the new rate folds back down as aliasing, as it does in a sampler without an input filter.
  5. Normalize (if on). The take is scaled so its peak sits at the full scale of the new bit depth, as you would set the input gain on a sampler.
  6. Bit depth. Each sample is rounded to the nearest step of the depth: 2b steps across the full range, so 65,536 at 16 bits, 4,096 at 12, 256 at 8 and 16 at 4. The rounding is mid-tread (silence stays exactly zero) and the top is clipped at one step under full scale. There is no dither and no noise shaping. Quiet parts of a 12-bit or 8-bit sound carry the steps in them.

Playing Back

Sounds below 44.1 kHz play back by zero-order hold (sample-and-hold). At the 44.1 kHz output, each sample of the sound is held until the next one is due, never blended into it. This keeps the stepped waveform and its images above the sound’s Nyquist frequency, as early samplers’ converters did. The hold’s own response is sin(πf/fs)/(πf/fs): −3.9 dB at the sound’s Nyquist frequency. Sounds at 44.1 kHz played at another pitch are interpolated linearly between samples. All voices are mixed in 32-bit float.

Pitch

Pitch speeds the input up by 0 to 24 semitones before it is sampled, and the pad it lands on is tuned down by the same amount, so it plays at its own pitch. This is the old trick of sampling a record at 45 instead of 33 and tuning it down: more sound fits in the same memory, and the lower effective rate and the tuning down change its character. With Pitch at +12 and a rate of 26.04 kHz, the sound is effectively sampled at 13.02 kHz.

Why a Buffer Helps

A guitar amp’s input presents about 1 MΩ to the guitar (“Hi-Z”). A line input like the MPC Sample’s presents much less, and a passive pickup loads into it: its resonant peak drops and flattens, and the sound comes out duller and weaker. This happens in the analog circuit before the converter. Software cannot undo it; Bright gets part of the way back, and Gain makes up the level, but a hardware buffer fixes it completely:

  • A buffered pedal between guitar and MPC. Most Boss pedals (a DS-1, an OD-1, a TU-3 tuner and so on) have a buffer that works even when the pedal is switched off. Plug the guitar into the pedal and the pedal’s output into the MPC.
  • A dedicated buffer pedal, or a DI box with a high-impedance input: the same result.
  • Active pickups (and basses with an active preamp) already have a low-impedance output, so they plug straight in with nothing lost.

With a buffer or active pickups, turn Bright down to 0: nothing needs putting back. Keep Gain to taste. Into the Input Effects chain, the amp models (Bassman, AC30, JTM45, DC30, JC-120, B-15) and the Speaker Sim then make a complete guitar or bass rig.

How the Two Modes Work

  • CYCLIC cuts the sound into cycles of a fixed length and repeats whole cycles (to make it longer) or drops them (to make it shorter), butted together. On a single-pitched sound with the cycle a whole number of its pitch periods, the joins line up and the result is clean; <AutoC> finds such a length by measuring the pitch. On anything else the joins show: that is the CYCLIC sound.
  • INTELL chooses its splice points: for each piece it searches nearby for the place where the sound best continues what the last piece was playing, and crossfades across the join. It suits speech, music and drum loops. Shorter pieces (high QUAL) follow the sound more closely; a wider crossfade (WIDTH) smooths the joins.

The Effects in Detail

Every effect has three controls, on K1–K3, each set from 0 to 127 like the knobs and shown in its own units. The effects run in stereo at 44.1 kHz. An effect named after a unit takes its character from it: how it clips, its EQ, how it moves.

In Print Effects they come in six categories, in this order: Reverb, EQ, Delay, Modulation, Drive/Dyn, Amp. In Input Effects any effect can go in any of the nine slots. In INPUT and PAD a chain holds at most one reverb and one delay.

As a send (TRACK/MIXER). Reverbs, delays and modulation add to the sound, so as sends only what they add comes back (the “wet” part). Drives, compressors, amps and EQs replace the sound, so as sends their whole output comes back, for parallel processing. A send of 0 is always silent and costs nothing.

MIDI Settings

SHIFT + Pad 8: Ext and USB MIDI, Base and Receive channels, Sync (send or receive MIDI clock), Local Mode, Soft Thru, and the sustain pedal (normal, to duration, or footswitch). <CC> opens the Mixer CC page: Mixer CC on or off, its Target (the bank’s 16 strips, or the selected track), and each control’s CC (Level, Pan, EQ Low, Mid and High, Mute, Solo), all off by default.

Learning CCs. Press B2 <Learn> and move a knob, fader or button on your controller. The selected control takes that CC, Mixer CC turns on so it answers at once, and the cursor moves to the next control. Press <Learn> again and move the next knob, and so on down the list. Pressed while the cursor is on Mixer CC or Target, <Learn> starts at Level. Press it again before moving anything to cancel. A CC can also be set with the wheel; below 0 is OFF. B3 <Default> sets every CC back to OFF.

<Notes> sets the note each pad sends and answers to: press a pad and turn the wheel, or <Learn> and play the note.

Additional Settings

B1 <Settings> on MIDI Settings opens Additional Settings:

  • Button Lights: red (default), white, pink or off.
  • Pad Lights: light blue, red, white or off (default). Lit pads dim while pressed, more with pressure.
  • Pad Thresh: the lightest pad hit that counts, 1 to 60 (default 12). Raise it if pads play by themselves; lower it to play very softly. A light hit that comes within 25 ms of a much harder one on another pad is taken as the panel shaking and ignored. A knob, slider or volume reading that jumps far from the last is ignored unless the next reading confirms it, so a stray reading cannot jump a setting or drop the volume.
  • Sequencer Res: MPC 3000, MPC60, MPC 2000 (all 96 PPQN) or SP12 (24 PPQN). See Chapter 4.
  • Screen: MPC3000 Blue (default), MPC60 Green, MPC1000 Green, MPC4000, MPC2000, Black/Red, Red/Black, Fairlight.
  • Start BPM: the tempo every sequence has at power-on (default 120.0; SHIFT with the wheel in tenths).
  • Start Bars: the length of every sequence at power-on (default 2).
  • Start TC: Timing Correct at power-on (default 1/16 NOTE).
  • Start Swing: swing at power-on (default 50%).

These and everything else here are kept on the card and come back at the next power-on. A first install starts with red button lights, pad lights off, MPC 3000 timing, the MPC3000 Blue screen, 120 BPM, 2 bars and 1/16 Timing Correct.

If Something Goes Wrong

  • A note will not stop: press STOP twice quickly to stop every sound.
  • The mixer does not answer MIDI CC: turn Mixer CC on and give each control a CC on the <CC> page.
  • The input cannot be heard: check Input Thru on Sample New Sound, and that Input Effects’ Input matches where you are plugged in.
  • Not sure what a page does: SHIFT + SAMPLE REC.
